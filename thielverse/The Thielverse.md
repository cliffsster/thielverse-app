## Eight beats

**1 — Stanford, 1987.** [[Peter Thiel]] founds the [[Stanford Review]]. It produces
[[David Sacks]], [[Keith Rabois]], [[Joe Lonsdale]] and [[Blake Masters]]. Thiel meets
[[Alex Karp]] at law school. The network selects for ideology years before any money exists.

**2 — PayPal, 1998–2002.** [[Confinity]] merges with [[Elon Musk]]'s [[X.com]]; eBay pays
$1.5B. Twenty people walk away rich, young, and owing each other favours.

**3 — Palantir, 2003.** Thiel points PayPal's fraud-detection logic at counterterrorism.
[[In-Q-Tel]] invests in 2005 and the [[Central Intelligence Agency]] becomes customer number
one — three years of CIA analysts shaping the product is what makes
[[Palantir Technologies]] real.

**4 — Founders Fund, 2005.** [[Luke Nosek]] and [[Ken Howery]] turn the friendship group into
permanent capital. It backs [[SpaceX]], [[Meta Platforms]], [[Anduril Industries]] — and funds
defence when nobody else will.

**5 — Anduril, 2017.** Palantir alumni run the play again in hardware.
[[Andreessen Horowitz]] arrives with [[Katherine Boyle]]'s American Dynamism thesis, and the
sector stops being contrarian.

**6 — Government, 2016–2026.** The network stops selling to the state and starts staffing it.
[[JD Vance]] becomes Vice President; [[David Sacks]] runs AI and crypto policy;
[[Jacob Helberg]] — a serving adviser to Palantir's CEO — becomes an under secretary of state.

**7 — Media, 2023–2025.** [[Tucker Carlson]] leaves [[Fox News]]. [[Bari Weiss]] sells
[[The Free Press]] to [[Paramount Skydance]] and takes over CBS News. The alternative media
becomes the incumbent media.

**8 — Fracture, 2025.** The coalition turns on itself: [[Steve Bannon]] against the tech right
over visas, [[Nick Fuentes]] and Carlson against [[AIPAC]] and the pro-Israel wing
[[Alex Karp]] stands on, [[Elon Musk]] against [[Donald Trump]], [[David Sacks]] against
[[Anthropic]] — and everyone against everyone over the [[Jeffrey Epstein]] files.
